In this incident, first published by ReversingLabs, dozens of NPM packages were found using variations of popular package names to deceive developers into including them in their applications. An application including one of these packages as a dependency will send users’ form data to attacker-controlled domains.
This attack was meticulously crafted and yielded impressive results with one of the packages reaching a total of 17,000 downloads. Since the targets of these attacks are end-users rather than developers, this number of downloads can suggest a much higher number of infections.
Details
The packages that were found share a similar modus operandi, their obfuscated code includes a feature that looks for form components on the website or application and sends the data filled in it to one of the attacker’s domains.
As it seems, these attacks were all carried out by the same actor with a preparation level surpassing what we commonly see in software supply chain attacks, marking another milestone in the field.
For example, the malicious package icon-package is trying to fool developers into using it instead of the legitimate popular ionicons, offering over 1300 icons for websites and application developers.
The user publishing this malicious package is ionic-io and the form data it exfiltrates is sent to hxxps://ionicio[.]com which is quite similar to the legitimate website of the package ionic.io and could be easily overlooked and passed as reliable to a developer looking into it. This attacker didn't stop there and also uploaded a similar-looking website on the malicious address to make it all seem reliable and innocent.

Other than this example, the attacker also targeted several other UI packages such as umbrellajs, swiper, and sidr, all with the same functionality of sending form data from end-users.
Conclusion
Typosquatting attacks are an efficient way to spread malicious packages to unsuspecting developers and in some cases, such as the one described in this blog, to their end-users.
The focus on end-users rather than developers separates this attack from most of what we see in recent times and makes it much more worrisome. in addition, the level of preparation for these attacks suggests it was done by a mature attacker that certainly possesses the skills to attack again and cause severe damage.
Checkmarx customers are protected from this threat and our research team is investigating further and will update should any new finding will come up.
