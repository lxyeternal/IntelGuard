In July, the software supply chain landscape was rattled by novel and sophisticated threats, from the first identified instance of a nation-state actor using open source to infiltrate the supply chains, to supply chain attacks specifically targeting the banking sector. New vulnerabilities were also unveiled in the open-source ecosystem providing additional methods for threat actors to hide their malware.
Let’s delve into some of the most striking events of July:
Lazarus Group Launches Open Source Supply Chain Attacks on Crypto Sector
The Lazarus Group, affiliated with North Korea, initiated open-source supply chain attacks targeting the cryptocurrency and gambling sectors. Utilizing deceptive tactics, they successfully tricked victims into using malicious open-source packages, highlighting an urgent need for developer awareness and vigilance. (report)
First Known Targeted OSS Supply Chain Attacks Against the Banking Sector
The Checkmarx Supply Chain research team identified targeted attacks against the banking sector, leveraging advanced techniques. The revelation emphasizes the urgent need to reassess controls in managing malicious packages. (report)
Manifest Confusion in NPM Ecosystem: A Hidden Danger
A significant bug, “Manifest Confusion,” was discovered in the npm ecosystem exposing a security gap where malware can lurk undetected. (report)
