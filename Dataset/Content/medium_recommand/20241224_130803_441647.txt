Throughout March, the open-source community faced several notable incidents. The NPM open-source ecosystem grappled with a massive spam campaign of unprecedented scale, involving hundreds of thousands of malicious package. The sheer volume of malicious uploads caused intermittent outages, creating periodic denial of service on the NPM platform. .NET developers encountered a new attack vector, where malicious actors exploited autorun mechanisms in NuGet packages for the first time. Moreover, the widely used 3CX Desktop App fell victim to a sophisticated, multi-stage supply chain attack. These incidents reveal an evolving landscape of threats and vulnerabilities in the software supply chain.
Let’s delve into some of the most striking events of March:
Massive SPAM Campaign Cripples NPM
Hundreds of thousands of malicious packages were uploaded to the NPM open-source ecosystem within days resulting in a sporadic DOS on NPM. These packages were part of various SPAM campaigns, including malware infections, referral scams, and crypto scams. The attackers were able to exploit the ecosystem’s positive reputation on search engines to effectively distribute their malicious links. (report)
New Attack Vector Observed, Targeting .NET Developers in A Software Supply Chain Attack
.NET developers faced a novel attack as malicious actors were seen for the first time exploiting autorun mechanisms in NuGet packages to spread their sophisticated payloads. The malicious packages contained a PowerShell script that executed upon installation, downloading, and executing a custom, sophisticated second-stage payload. Some of the malicious packages attempted to conceal their intent by using hidden dependencies. (report)
3CX Desktop App Weaponized to Target Company’s Customers
The 3CX Desktop App, a popular communication tool utilized across multiple platforms such as Windows, macOS, Linux, and mobile devices, fell victim to a complex, multi-stage supply chain attack. With over 600,000 customer companies and 12 million daily users, the 3CX Desktop App is a critical communication tool for many. The attackers managed to weaponize the app, resulting in sensitive data exposure and theft for both individual users and organizations, leaving a massive impact on the app’s extensive user base. (report)
