Welcome to the fourth in a series of blog posts where we will be delving into the dark art of deception on GitHub, a trusted platform that can, unfortunately, be manipulated for nefarious purposes.
Part one of this series discussed how someone can create a false sense of credibility by associating themselves with a reputable organization.
Part two of this series discussed how someone can create a false impression of relentless activity over a long period of time.
Part three of this series discussed how someone can add well-known contributors to their commits.
In this latest post, we will demonstrate yet another deceitful strategy: How threat actors decorate their profile with GitHub achievements.
These badges, often seen as symbols of proficiency and dedication, can fool unsuspecting victims into believing that they’re dealing with a seasoned GitHub user who has accomplished significant feats or, at the very least, has a track record of substantial activity and contributions.
Unfortunately, with the assistance of the GitHub API and some basic automation, anyone can accumulate all the badges within a matter of moments or achieve the coveted golden level within an hour.
