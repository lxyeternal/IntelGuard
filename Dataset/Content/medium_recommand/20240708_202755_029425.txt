The year 2022 saw a significant rise in cyberattacks. Out of these, it’s estimated 40% of these are targeting the software supply chain. Accenture estimates losses due to general supply chain disruptions at €112.7 billion, or 0.9% of GDP in the Eurozone.
Fortunately, software developers have not been sitting idle. In the last 18 months, the CNCF Security Tag published the 2nd version of its white paper on cloud native security as well as on cloud native supply chain security. Heck, even the White House got interested and Oracle was there. Cosign, part of the Sigstore project, also reached its 1.0 milestone. Subsequently, the Kubernetes community announced its adoption of Sigstore for signing artifacts and verifying signatures.
If you are doing cloud native on OCI, how do you make use of this?

Enforcing the use of signed images
Now that we have signed our container images, we need to enforce the use of signed images. In OKE, there are a couple of ways to achieve this:

Using OKE itself: at creation time, OKE allows you to specify a list of image signing keys that you can store in OCI KMS. In order for pods to be successfully created, the container images used need to be signed with those image signing keys.

Summary
In this short article, we have shown a few things:

Improving your software supply chain security on OCI by using images signed by the cosign
Storing your container image and the signature in OCI Container Registry
Using Kyverno to create Kubernetes Policies, in this case, to enforce the deployment of container images signed with certain keys by verifying the signature stored in OCIR
Obviously, we are barely scratching the surface of cloud native security here but if you are running on OCI and using OKE and you’ve been looking at improving your software supply chain, I hope you find this article useful enough to get started.
