In May, the open-source community confronted diverse threats. Incidents ranged from the hijacking of an S3 bucket serving malicious binaries, to mass scanning of GitHub repositories for CI misconfigurations. New vulnerabilities also arose with AI ‘hallucinating’ non-existent open-source packages while attackers were found exploiting abandoned package names and spreading malware across different languages. Furthermore, PyPI had to temporarily halt all new user registrations and project submissions.
Let’s delve into some of the most striking events of May:
Hijacking S3 Buckets: New Attack Technique Exploited in the Wild by Supply Chain Attackers
Attackers hijacked an abandoned S3 bucket used for serving binaries to the NPM package “bignum”, and replaced the legitimate binaries with malicious ones that steal user credentials and system information and exfiltrating them to the hijacked bucket. This attack highlights the danger of relying on software dependencies that deliver other compiled parts in build time and the need for vigilance in supply chain processes. (report)
Mass Scanning of Popular GitHub Repos for CI Misconfiguration
A GitHub user named pastramahodu forked nearly 2,000 repositories with a total of 319,191 stars, as part of a seemingly widespread bug bounty hunt for misconfigurations in popular project’s CI pipelines. This automated procedure involved creating Pull Requests that triggered the CI pipelines and executed custom commands, highlighting the increasing focus on pipeline security as an entry point for supply chain attacks. (report)
Trusting AI-Generated Code: Determining Reality from Hallucination
Recent research has shown that AI language models like ChatGPT may sometimes ‘hallucinate’ nonexistent open-source packages. This is a potential vulnerability that malicious actors could exploit by fabricating malicious packages inspired by AI-generated, fictitious ones, thereby infiltrating the development environment with harmful code. (report)
Malicious Packages Linked Across Languages and the Dangers of Abandoned Open-Source Dependencies
A recent investigation by Mend uncovered a case involving an abandoned RubyGems package, claimed by an unidentified entity posing a risk for projects that have not updated their dependencies. Furthermore, the discovery of related malicious packages in NPM, Pypi and Ruby, indicates a tactic by attackers to proliferate their malware across multiple programming languages, broadening their potential impact. (report)
PyPi Temporarily Suspends All New Users and Projects due to an Overwhelm in Malicious Activity
Over the weekend of May 20th, the PyPi team temporarily suspended the creation of new users and publication of new projects due to a high volume of detected malicious activity that they were unable to respond to in a timely manner. (report)
