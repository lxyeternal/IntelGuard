Welcome to the third of a series of blog posts, where we will be delving into the dark art of deception on GitHub, a trusted platform that can, unfortunately, be manipulated for nefarious purposes.
In part one of this series, we wrote about how someone can create a false sense of credibility by associating themselves with a reputable organization.
In part two of this series, we wrote about how someone can create a false impression of relentless activity over a long period of time.
In this post, we will demonstrate how someone with nefarious motives can add well-known contributors to their commits, meaning, they could use the reputation of popular and credible contributors on GitHub and push a commit on their behalf, thus adding an additional layer of credibility.
All these manipulations blend into the general pool of commits, and distinguishing the verified from the unverified becomes nearly impossible from an outsider’s perspective.
Spoofing commits to make it appear as if they were created by any user we choose is quite simple:
Step 1: Discover the user’s Email address:
Although GitHub provides mechanisms to conceal a user’s email, these features are opt-in and often overlooked by users. To uncover a user’s email address, one can manually follow or automate these steps:

Identify an authentic commit from the user using this URL pattern: https://github.com/<username>/<repo_name>/commit/<commit_hash>
Add “.patch” at the end of the URL, like this: https://github.com/<username>/<repo_name>/commit/<commit_hash>.patch
Visiting this address will yield a result that includes the user’s email address.
