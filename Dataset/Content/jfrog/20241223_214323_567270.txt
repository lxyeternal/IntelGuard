What causes tool sprawl?
Tool sprawl occurs when teams use too many tools to complete a task. This typically happens over time as new toolsets get added to tackle unique problems a dev team is facing. Sprawl can lead to complexity, fragmentation, and gaps in security and automation across the software supply chain.  With multiple point solutions to manage, it can be difficult to keep track of what’s happening across your software pipelines. It also requires a heavy lift from DevOps and IT teams to maintain custom connections between all the tools.
Problems caused by tool sprawl
When it comes to tool sprawl, there are several potential issues that can arise:

Patchwork Security: Point solutions may not provide complete protection and add complexity as well as potential weaknesses to security processes.
Fragile Custom Connections: DevOps and IT teams must ensure that a wide range of tools work smoothly together. Any unexpected change can disrupt integrations and automations.
Ballooning IT Costs: Every extra tool impacts the total cost of ownership (TCO), not just license fees. There are also infrastructure, upkeep, training and integration costs.
Unknown Unknowns: Shadow IT might help Devs in the short-term, but poses risks in the long-term. If tools aren’t monitored, they can’t be secured or automated.
Areas to consolidate
Consolidating your tool stack is beneficial for improving performance and reducing complexity. Here are some potential areas to focus on:

Artifact storage
Source code and VCS
Container registries
Software security solutions
Cloud/dynamic runtime deployment technologies
CI/CD tooling
Implementing a single platform to monitor multiple parts of your systems and processes
Consolidating these areas can help streamline processes, reduce the time spent managing multiple tools, provide insight into development gaps, improve velocity, and reduce the risk of downtime.
A five-step plan for consolidating your DevOps tools
Finding the right tools to consolidate and manage your DevOps environment can be daunting. To ensure optimal efficiency and maximize cost savings, it’s essential to have a plan in place. Follow this five-step plan for successful consolidation and implementation:

Create a DevOps and Security Tools Inventory: Begin generating a comprehensive inventory of which tools are used for source code and version control management, builds, and deployments.
Identify what matters most: Analyze the inventory to determine which tools to consolidate or replace. Which tools have the biggest impact on the team versus those playing a niche role?
Decide which focus areas to keep, combine, or phase out: Now that you have a list of tools, it’s time to identify which areas are most important to either consolidate around or have a dedicated tool for.
Use vendors that bring tools together to speed software updates: It’s unlikely that any one tool is going to meet all of your needs in a given functional area. Research the vendor’s roadmap to see how their solutions will evolve over time. Also, look for vendors that play nice with others. For example, do they offer robust APIs or have OOTB integrations with the other tools you need to leverage?
Set up a plan to migrate to a consolidated set of DevOps tools: Now that you’ve chosen which tools to consolidate, it’s time to create a plan for migrating your datasets from each. Be sure to involve the right stakeholders and establish an appropriate timeline based on how embedded a tool may be in current processes.
