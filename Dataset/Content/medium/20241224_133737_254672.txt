Welcome to the second of a series of blog posts, where we will be delving into the dark art of deception on GitHub, a trusted platform that can, unfortunately, be manipulated for nefarious purposes.
This time we will focus on an intriguing facet: commit spoofing. Attackers employ this technique to create the illusion of activity, effectively making their deceptive profiles appear trustworthy to the untrained eye.
In the hunt for an open-source project, developers often gravitate towards active and well-maintained ones. GitHub’s metadata makes it simple to evaluate these factors. Unfortunately, the ease with which some of this information can be manipulated opens the door to deceit.
Astoundingly, spoofing activity on GitHub is all too simple. With a few simple steps, attackers can present a picture of constant activity, creating the image of a dedicated developer who seemingly works around the clock.
Creating a false impression of relentless activity over a long period is feasible due to GitHub’s lack of verification for commit timestamps. The user’s activity graph on their profile page, a heatmap reflecting their activity over time, can be filled with bogus activity, making the attacker appear legitimate.
All it takes to manipulate the timestamp is altering two environment variables on the local machine with the command “git set” when pushing the commits. This makes the commits appear as if they were performed at a different time rather than the time they were actually committed.
image: D:/Desktop/malware/IntelliGraph\Dataset\Image\medium\20241224_133737_254672\0m3594XO69S2sOFw.png
It’s critical to note that this falsified data can predate both the user committing it and the repository it was committed to. And as these graphs also display activity from private repositories, it becomes incredibly challenging to spot these fakes.
Additionally, GitHub does offer a feature to verify commits, which is intended to provide an extra layer of assurance. The challenge here, though, is distinguishing between verified and unverified commits. To an outsider’s eye, they look identical. This lack of differentiation complicates the issue, providing attackers with another loophole to exploit. Without an easy way to identify verified commits, this security feature loses some of its efficacy, allowing the deceit to persist undetected.

Remember, the world of cybersecurity is filled with illusions.
