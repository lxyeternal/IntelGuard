Welcome to the Fifth in a series of blog posts where we will be delving into the dark art of deception on GitHub, a trusted platform that can, unfortunately, be manipulated for nefarious purposes.
Part one of this series discussed how someone can create a false sense of credibility by associating themselves with a reputable organization.
Part two of this series discussed how someone can create a false impression of relentless activity over a long period of time.
Part three of this series discussed how someone can add well-known contributors to their commits.
Part four of this series discussed how threat actors decorate their profile with GitHub achievements.
In this latest post, we will demonstrate yet another deceitful strategy: How threat actors manipulate their GitHub profile to showcase extraordinary stats about themselves.
Stats In Your Profile
When you create a GitHub repository with the same name as your username, the README.md file from this repository is prominently displayed on your profile page. This space is utilized by many users to showcase various personal statistics.
However, the lack of any verification mechanism means the authenticity of these statistics remains unconfirmed.
After delving into the range of artificial enhancement methods over the past five posts, it is crucial to understand that these techniques, while seemingly beneficial for showcasing a novice GitHub account as extraordinary, the same mechanisms can be exploited by attackers. Therefore, exercise caution when granting maintainer access to your GitHub repository to unknown entities or when incorporating their code into your projects.
Remember, not all is as it seems…
