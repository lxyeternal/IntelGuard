The ripple effects of the supply chain attack, which impacted the 3CX DesktopApp, a popular voice and video conferencing application, continue to send shockwaves across the cybersecurity landscape as new information emerges about the scope and severity of this sophisticated cyber assault.
It has been revealed that critical infrastructure organizations in the United States and Europe have been affected by this wide-ranging attack, with the potential impact on many more organizations becoming a pressing concern.
The North Korean-backed hacking group UNC4736, attributed with high confidence to the Lazarus Group, has been linked to this cascading software supply chain compromise.
A Cascade of Supply Chain Attacks
The initial intrusion vector for the 3CX attack has been traced back to an employee’s personal computer, compromised after downloading and installing a trojanized version of Trading Technologies’ X_TRADER software. Astoundingly, the X_TRADER software itself had already been compromised in a separate supply chain attack, making the 3CX incident a rare and sophisticated example of a cascading software supply chain compromise, where one supply chain attack sets the stage for another.
The malware deployed in this attack, VEILEDSIGNAL, granted the threat actors administrator-level access and persistence on the compromised system. The attackers moved laterally through the 3CX environment, eventually compromising both the Windows and macOS build environments. The malware components used in the attack, TAXHAUL launcher, and COLDCAT downloader on the Windows build environment, as well as the POOLRAT backdoor on the macOS build server, enabled the attackers to maintain persistence and control over the compromised systems. (We describe the complete breakdown of this attack in our previous blog.)
Conclusion
While the full extent of the damage caused by this supply chain attack is still unknown, it is clear that the implications are far-reaching. The attackers have demonstrated a highly effective method for compromising organizations, and the potential for similar attacks in the future cannot be ruled out.
In response to these revelations, companies must prioritize supply chain security and implement robust measures to mitigate the risk of such attacks.
Moreover, organizations should be cautious about the software installed on employees’ personal devices, as these can serve as an entry point for threat actors.
