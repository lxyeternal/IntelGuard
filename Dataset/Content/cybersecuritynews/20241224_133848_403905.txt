
Gartner is, without a doubt, one of the leading voices in the cybersecurity industry. When they talk, we should listen. In May of this year, the tech consultancy released a report entitled “Voice of the Customer for Data Security Posture Management” that synthesizes 379 Gartner peer reviews – customer-generated product reviews – to offer insights into the data security posture management (DSPM) market. So, let’s explore what it tells us about DSPM and how it can enhance your organization’s data security posture.
Cloud Infrastructure and Data Security Posture: The Story So Far
In the past few years, the increasing adoption of cloud infrastructure has brought enormous benefits for organizations of all shapes and sizes. Cloud service providers (CSPs) allow, for example, Netflix to handle traffic spikes without worrying about server capacity, Coca-Cola to conduct in-depth data analysis, and NASA to reduce energy consumption. However, these benefits come at a price.
The problem is that expansive cloud infrastructure compromises data security postures, especially compared to the on-premises infrastructures of yesteryear. Already overstretched security teams are struggling to keep track of and, hence, protect data and data repositories – also known as shadow data – across multiple CSP platforms.
To make matters worse, many of the leading data security products, including data loss prevention and endpoint prevention, are siloed. They fail to integrate or share policies properly, resulting in further gaps and inconsistencies in data protection. This is where DSPM comes in.
Data Security Posture Management: The Gartner Definition
According to Gartner’s Voice of the Customer for Data Security Posture Management report, DSPM solutions assess data security, identify and classify risks, implement security controls, and track and update the security posture to maintain business confidentiality.
They provide visibility into where sensitive data is, who has access to it, how it has been used, and the security posture of the data store or application. DSPM solutions achieve this by assessing the current state of data security, identifying and classifying potential risks and vulnerabilities, implementing security controls to mitigate these risks, and regularly monitoring and updating the security posture to ensure it remains effective.
DSPM enables businesses to maintain the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of sensitive data. Its typical users include information technology (IT) departments, security teams, compliance teams, and executive leadership.
Key Benefits of DSPM Tools
Now that we better understand what DSPM is and who it is for, we can explore some of the benefits of these solutions. Of course, the primary benefit is improving data security postures, but let’s get a little more specific. Organizations that implement DSPMs will achieve improved:

Data Discovery: Security teams can use DSPM solutions to uncover unknown or shadow data repositories to ensure all data assets are inventoried and protected.
Risk Management: DSPM tools map data flows and pipelines to help security teams assess and mitigate data exposure and misconfiguration risks.
Compliance: DSPM tools provide visibility into data residency and access controls, streamlining compliance processes for data protection regulations.
Automation: DSPM solutions ease burdens on overstretched security teams by automating laborious tasks like data discovery, classification, and risk assessment processes.
Integration: By integrating seamlessly with existing security infrastructure, DSPM tools provide organizations with a unified, holistic approach to data protection across cloud and on-premises environments.
DSPM: The Future of Data Protection
The key takeaway from Gartner’s report, however, is that DSPM will shape the future of data protection. Although it is a relatively new market (Gartner first coined the term DSPM in 2022), peer reviews for the industry’s leading solutions are remarkably positive, suggesting the market has matured quickly.
The vendors included in Gartner’s report – Cyera, OneTrust, Palo Alto Networks (Dig Security), Rubrik (Laminar), Securiti, Symmetry Systems, and Varonis – all score over 80% in the willingness to recommend category and over four stars in the product capabilities, sales, deployment, and support categories. It’s clear that, despite its relative infancy, the DSPM market is in a pretty good position.
While impressive, it’s unsurprising that the DSPM market has matured as it has. Cybersecurity threats are growing more sophisticated, cloud environments more complicated, and security teams more overstretched: it stands to reason that any solution that addresses these concerns would be successful. Gartner themselves share this opinion, predicting in 2023’s Innovation Insight report that by 2026, over 20% of businesses will prioritize DSPM technologies and use them to discover and secure their data repositories, both known and unknown.
Is DSPM Right for Your Organization?
That said, DSPM won’t be for everyone. DSPM is ideal for organizations with large amounts of sensitive data stored in complex cloud environments that require rigorous visibility, access control, and risk management – especially in regulated industries like finance and healthcare. It’s less suitable for small businesses without complex data security needs or those with limited budgets, as the investment may outweigh the benefits. Purchasing DSPM must not be a rash decision – consider your unique needs and the Gartner Voice of the Customer Report before purchasing.
